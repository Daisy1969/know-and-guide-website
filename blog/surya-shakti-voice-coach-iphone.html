Building a Voice-Guided Surya Shakti Practice Companion

Posted: July 19, 2026   •   Category: Yoga Technology

I have been developing a private, native iPhone application to support my daily Surya Shakti practice. The purpose is simple: let the phone announce each movement in English and then Sanskrit, wait for me to repeat the Sanskrit name, and only then continue to the next movement.

The application is designed as a personal practice companion for someone who has already learned the process correctly. It is not intended to teach Surya Shakti, replace a qualified teacher, alter the practice, or provide independent yoga instruction.

How the prototype works

English, then SanskritEach movement is spoken first in English and then by its Sanskrit name.
Listen and waitA short beep signals that the microphone is ready, and the app waits for the Sanskrit response.
Gentle correctionTwo beeps request another attempt. After two missed attempts, the Sanskrit name is spoken again before listening resumes.
AirPods supportThe microphone, spoken guidance, voice test, and playback test can use compatible AirPods or the iPhone.
Practice trackingThe app records completed cycles, practice days, current streak, longest streak, and lifetime totals on the device.
Pronunciation reflectionAt the end of a session, optional AI feedback summarises which Sanskrit names may need more practice.

Why I built it

During practice, I wanted a simple hands-free rhythm: hear the movement, move into the posture, repeat the Sanskrit name, and continue without touching the screen. The application keeps the established 18-position sequence unchanged while helping me remember the names and observe my consistency over time.

The current build is a standalone SwiftUI iPhone application rather than a website. It runs from its own app icon, stores practice records locally, supports a manual “I said it” control when speech recognition struggles, and can use optional cloud voice and transcription services for improved recognition.

Current status: This is a private personal prototype. It is not an official Isha Foundation application, it is not endorsed by Isha Foundation, and it is not currently available for public download.

Approval before any public release

Before making the application available to other people, I intend to seek written approval from Isha Foundation. Only if that approval is granted would I consider offering the iPhone application free of charge.

I would also follow any guidance provided about naming, presentation, pronunciation, teaching boundaries, intellectual property, privacy, safety, and whether an application of this kind should be released at all.

Official reference: Learn more from Isha Foundation’s article, Classical Yoga: Surya Kriya and Surya Shakti.

Respecting the practice

The technology is intended to support memory and pronunciation, not to reinterpret the practice. Anyone interested in learning Surya Shakti should use official Isha Foundation information and receive appropriate instruction rather than relying on an app or an online sequence guide.

I will continue testing the private version on my own iPhone, particularly the AirPods microphone route, Sanskrit speech recognition, spoken prompts, repetition logic, cycle counting, and end-of-session feedback.
