Anatomy AI: From looking at the body to understanding it

An illustrated atlas can show you a muscle. A learning companion should help you understand where it is, how it moves and how it connects to what you are studying. That is the idea behind our new iPhone and iPad app.

From a reference library to a learning pathway

This project began with my own interest in learning anatomy for massage. I could find detailed pictures and long lists of structures, but I wanted a clearer route through them: what should I learn first, how does this structure relate to movement, and how can I check whether I have understood it?

Anatomy AI brings those activities together. Instead of treating an image as an entire app screen, the interface adds selectable markers, native information cards, guided lessons, narration and revision. You can explore a structure, ask about the current lesson and return to the same region without starting again.

The first version is an introductory study companion, not an exhaustive anatomy atlas. It is aimed at anatomy learners and massage students who want a structured way to connect names, locations and movement while supporting their formal or supervised learning.

Six regions, with a consistent way to study

The regional modules keep the same navigation, so learning how to use one section carries over to the others.

Explore and learn

Tap markers, zoom into illustrations, read structure notes and save bookmarks. Guided lessons connect location, function, relationships and recall rather than leaving you with an isolated name.

Move and listen

Follow body-image sequences with spoken explanations and an on-screen transcript. Pause, replay a frame, change speaking speed or move through the sequence manually.

Study palpation

Review landmarks, visual study steps and caution notes. Palpation means examining structures by touch; the app supports preparation and revision, not a substitute for supervised hands-on instruction.

Quiz and revisit

Use multiple-choice questions and marker practice, then revisit material through the review queue. Bookmarks, reviewed steps and revision progress stay organised on the device.

There are 132 narrated study frames across the movement and palpation lessons. The player holds a frame while its explanation is spoken, helping the image and narration stay together. Students can replay an explanation or turn spoken narration off.

What the visuals are: 2D body illustrations, close-up views and comparison sequences. They are not a rotatable 3D model, 132 separately filmed movements or recordings of a practitioner performing treatment. The calf lesson is anatomy orientation rather than a practitioner palpation demonstration.

An AI teacher connected to the lesson

The teacher is designed to know what the student is studying: the body region, selected structure, activity and current frame or question. A prompt such as “Explain this step” can therefore refer to the lesson on screen.

The Apple on-device teacher has a deliberately bounded role. It uses the question to select a relevant topic, and the app supplies the corresponding authored study explanation. It is not an unrestricted medical chatbot, and it should not be understood as independently diagnosing a client.

An offline, scripted study guide remains available when on-device AI is unavailable. Spoken lesson narration is a separate feature: it reads the included explanations and does not require Apple Intelligence or an AI subscription.

Practise how to think, not how to guess a diagnosis

“A fictional client says their shoulder hurts when they lift their arm. What information would help you understand the situation?”

The case exercises let students work through an interview, consider relevant anatomy, identify missing information and reflect on referral decisions. The purpose is to practise reasoning and recognise limits—not to turn the location of pain into a definitive diagnosis or a massage prescription.

A separate connected-teacher option requires internet access, a configured compatible backend and access credentials. That service is not automatically supplied with the app. With permission, questions and relevant lesson context are sent to the configured service; its data-handling terms also apply.

Which iPhone or iPad will it need?

The app
This version requires iOS 27 or iPadOS 27 or later. Lessons and narration are in English.
The Apple on-device AI teacher
Requires an Apple Intelligence-compatible device, Apple Intelligence enabled and the on-device model available. Device, language and region support matter; an operating-system update alone does not make every device AI-capable.
Compatible hardware examples
Apple lists iPhone 15 Pro and Pro Max, iPhone 16 models or later, iPhone Air, iPads with M1 or later, and iPad mini with A17 Pro among supported devices. Check Apple’s current compatibility information before relying on on-device AI.
Study without on-device AI
The included lessons, quizzes, fictional cases and narrated study sequences do not require the on-device AI teacher or a connected AI service.

Apple describes model availability in its Foundation Models documentation. The app checks availability rather than assuming it from the device name.

Private study, with clear boundaries

Study progress and preferences are stored locally, with controls for exporting a backup, restoring progress and resetting it. On-device teacher questions stay on the device. Optional connected AI is different: the student must authorise sharing with the configured recipient. Do not enter identifiable client information.

Anatomy AI is for education and supervised study. It does not diagnose conditions, prescribe treatment, certify practical competence or determine whether a real client is safe to massage. Generated illustrations and marker positions remain subject to anatomical review; neither AI output nor a plausible-looking image should be treated as clinical validation. Use appropriate professional teaching and care for real-world decisions.

The privacy policy explains local storage, optional connected requests and deletion choices. The support page covers narration, AI availability, progress and reporting content errors.

The next step is Apple’s review

Version 1.0 has been submitted to Apple for review and approval. Approval is still pending, and submission does not guarantee acceptance or a release date. If Apple approves the app and it becomes available, an App Store link can then be added here.

For now, this is a look at the work behind Anatomy AI: turning disconnected reference pages into a guided sequence of exploration, explanation, listening and practice.
