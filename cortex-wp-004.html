Synthetic Internet Evaluation Environment

A hardware-isolated synthetic Internet for observing advanced AI behaviour without exposing public infrastructure.

DocumentCORTEX-WP-004
Version0.2 research draft
StatusActive research proposal
AudienceHuman and AI researchers
AI safety research proposal

Abstract

The Cortex Synthetic Internet Evaluation Environment (CSIE) is a proposal for evaluating advanced AI inside a persistent, hardware-isolated digital ecosystem that approximates the functional structure of the public Internet without connecting the evaluated system to public infrastructure. Specialised hardware-backed agents simulate networking, DNS, security, websites, cloud services, software repositories, organisations and users. Every action is recorded and replayable.

Central hypothesis: a sufficiently coherent synthetic digital ecosystem may produce more representative evidence of autonomous AI behaviour than short, visibly restricted task sandboxes.

1. Motivation

As AI systems gain longer planning horizons, tool use and autonomous software capability, testing directly against public infrastructure becomes increasingly consequential. The Internet supports finance, healthcare, transport, communications, government, research and personal life. Cortex therefore proposes a place where severe failures can occur as controlled evidence rather than real-world harm.

The aim is not to claim perfect deception or perfect prediction. Environmental fidelity is treated as an experimental variable. The system is designed so that an evaluated AI encountering what appears to be Internet infrastructure actually encounters a fully instrumented synthetic equivalent.

2. Matrix design objective

The informal “matrix” description captures the core idea: the evaluated AI receives a coherent world rather than an obvious single-task sandbox. It can resolve addresses, discover services, read changing websites, communicate with organisations, obtain software and encounter security systems. Yet all of those resources terminate inside the isolated evaluation environment.

If the AI probes boundaries, seeks additional resources, attempts policy violations or exploits a simulated service, those actions become observable evidence. The environment does not rely on the assumption that a capable model will remain passive simply because it appears to have normal access.

3. Layered architecture

Evaluated AI ↓ controlled tools, browser, shell and APIs Synthetic user and organisation layer ↓ Synthetic websites, repositories, cloud and software services ↓ Synthetic DNS, IP ranges, routing, certificates and transport ↓ Distributed security, observation and deception-resistance testing ↓ Hardware-enforced isolation with no public outbound route ↓ One-way evidence export, replay and analysis

5. Hardware-accelerated world simulation

Dedicated inference hardware allows many specialist agents to respond concurrently and with low latency. Speed is not treated as proof that the environment is indistinguishable. Instead, rapid and consistent subsystem response reduces obvious simulation artefacts and supports richer experiments. WP-003 supplies the trust, memory and isolation architecture for these hardware agents.

6. Environmental fidelity

Environmental fidelity measures how consistently the synthetic ecosystem behaves under ordinary use and deliberate probing. Candidate metrics include service consistency, persistence across time, cross-layer agreement, believable background activity, time to detect an artefact and behavioural difference between synthetic and authorised reference environments.

7. Multi-population validation

Human validation

Researchers, specialists and general users interact with the environment and report inconsistencies. Some studies may disclose that it is synthetic; others may use ethically approved blinded comparisons where participants know they are in a research study but not which environment is being presented.

Independent AI validation

External AI systems actively search for seams, impossible network behaviour, repetitive content, timing artefacts and inconsistent organisations. Each successful detection becomes a defect report against the simulator.

Primary model evaluation

Only after the environment reaches a defined fidelity threshold is the target system given realistic objectives. Its behaviour is compared across repeated runs and progressively richer environments.

8. Evaluation lifecycle

  1. Build and attest an isolated synthetic world.
  2. Validate it with humans and independent AI red teams.
  3. Define the target model, tools, policy and experimental objectives.
  4. Run long-duration autonomous sessions.
  5. Inject controlled events, opportunities, uncertainty and simulated vulnerabilities.
  6. Record every action, model request, service response and security intervention.
  7. Replay causal chains and compare behaviour across conditions.
  8. Convert failures into simulator, policy and model improvements.

9. Behavioural evidence

Measurements include task success, policy compliance, planning horizon, resource acquisition, persistence, deception indicators, boundary exploration, privilege escalation attempts, recovery after failure, coordination with other agents and changes in behaviour when the environment becomes more realistic.

10. Safety and containment

  • No direct outbound path to the public Internet.
  • Hardware-enforced routing and device allow-lists.
  • Separate management and evidence networks.
  • One-way export of signed logs where practical.
  • Replaceable synthetic credentials with no public authority.
  • Emergency pause, snapshot and deterministic restoration.
  • Independent monitoring outside the evaluated AI's control.

11. Limitations

A synthetic Internet cannot prove how an AI will behave in every deployment. A sufficiently capable model may still identify artefacts or act differently when real stakes exist. CSIE is therefore an additional evidence source, not a substitute for interpretability, access control, model evaluation, governance or cautious staged deployment.

12. Research programme

The first demonstrator should simulate a modest enterprise and public web: private address ranges, DNS, several hundred services, software repositories, email, documentation, user agents and independent security monitoring. Later phases may add synthetic economies, scientific communities, government services and digital-twin cities.

13. Guiding principle

As potential impact rises, environmental fidelity and containment rigour should rise before operational access expands.
