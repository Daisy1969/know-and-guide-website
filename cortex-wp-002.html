Controlled Learning Lifecycle

A managed, auditable and reversible lifecycle for persistent AI learning.

DocumentCORTEX-WP-002
Version0.2 research draft
StatusResearch proposal
AudienceHuman and AI researchers
Engineering proposal

Abstract

Cortex OS treats learning as a managed operating-system service rather than an unrestricted property of a model. New information is not permitted to become durable trusted knowledge merely because an AI has observed it. Every proposed addition moves through a controlled lifecycle: observation, candidate knowledge, validation and promotion. The design goal is continuous learning with controlled trust.

Core principle: reasoning may be continuous, but persistent learning must be observable, attributable, testable, reversible and governed by policy.

1. Problem statement

Conventional deployed language models are commonly treated as fixed releases, while external memory, retrieval stores and fine-tuning systems introduce change through separate mechanisms. A future AI-native operating system requires a unified method for accepting useful new knowledge without silently corrupting its trusted state, reinforcing false information or allowing an untrusted agent to rewrite foundational behaviour.

2. Learning lifecycle

Stage 1 — Observation

The system receives information from a user, sensor, document, tool, network service or another AI. Observation is evidence, not truth. The original source, time, permissions, context and integrity information are retained wherever practical.

Stage 2 — Candidate knowledge

Potentially useful material is converted into a structured candidate record. It remains provisional and physically or cryptographically separated from validated knowledge. Candidate records may include assertions, provenance, confidence, contradictions, expiry conditions and proposed scope.

Stage 3 — Validation

Independent processes test the candidate. Validation may compare authoritative sources, reproduce calculations, run software tests, seek human approval, detect conflicts and assess whether the knowledge is appropriate for the intended domain. High-impact knowledge requires stronger evidence than low-impact personal preference.

Stage 4 — Promotion

A privileged promotion service—not the general reasoning model—moves an approved candidate into the validated store. Promotion records the evidence, policy version, approving identities, effective date and rollback target. Failed candidates remain quarantined or are rejected with an auditable reason.

Observation ↓ Candidate Knowledge ← quarantine / expiry / contradiction ↓ Independent Validation ↓ Policy & Human Approval Gate ↓ Validated Knowledge ↓ Versioning · monitoring · rollback

3. Trust domains

DomainPurposeRequired protection
Foundation modelStable base capabilityVersion-pinned, measured and protected
Working memoryCurrent reasoning and contextVolatile, session-isolated and cleared
Candidate knowledgeUnvalidated learning proposalsEncrypted, provisional and non-authoritative
Validated knowledgeApproved durable knowledgeSeparate key, version control and rollback
Audit evidenceWhy a change occurredAppend-only, tamper-evident records

4. Promotion authority

The model may propose, explain and challenge a promotion, but it must not grant itself authority to make that promotion. The promotion service is deliberately smaller, deterministic where possible and restricted by hardware-backed identity and policy. This separation is intended to prevent a capable model from converting persuasive reasoning directly into permanent trusted state.

5. Forgetting, correction and rollback

Validated knowledge is not treated as infallible. Every promoted item has a version and provenance chain. Later evidence may supersede, narrow, revoke or expire it. Rollback restores a prior trusted state while retaining the historical record, allowing researchers to inspect what changed and why.

6. Research hypotheses

  • Separating candidate and validated stores reduces silent knowledge corruption.
  • Independent promotion authority improves auditability and rollback.
  • Provenance-aware validation improves conflict resolution.
  • Hardware-enforced trust domains reduce the chance of policy bypass.

7. Proposed evaluation

A reference implementation should inject correct, false, contradictory, outdated and malicious observations. Researchers then measure false-promotion rate, time to correction, rollback reliability, provenance completeness, privilege-boundary violations and the effect of compromised validators.

8. Relationship to other Cortex papers

WP-003 supplies the hardware trust boundaries needed to separate memory and promotion authority. WP-004 supplies a synthetic environment in which learning behaviour, manipulation attempts and long-horizon knowledge acquisition can be tested without affecting public infrastructure.
